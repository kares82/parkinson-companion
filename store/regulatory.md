# Regulatory position (EU MDR / UKCA / FDA) — read before writing marketing copy

Not legal advice. This is the reasoning to take to a regulatory consultant, and
the line to stay on until you have.

## Why the app is currently outside medical-device scope

Under EU **Regulation 2017/745 (MDR)**, software is a medical device when the
manufacturer intends it for a medical purpose — diagnosis, prevention,
monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease.

MDCG 2019-11 draws the line at what the software *does to* the data. Software
that only **stores, archives, communicates or performs simple search** on data
the user entered is **not** a medical device. Software that **interprets** data,
or creates new clinical information, generally **is** (usually Class IIa under
Rule 11).

Parkinson Companion, as built, only records and totals what the user typed:
counts, sums, medians, a bar chart, a percentage. It performs no interpretation,
produces no diagnosis, suggests no dose, and predicts nothing.

**Intended purpose statement — keep this exact framing:**

> Parkinson Companion is a diary that allows a person with Parkinson's disease
> or their carer to record and review their own observations, and to share that
> record with a healthcare professional. It does not diagnose, does not
> recommend or calculate treatment, and does not replace clinical judgement.

## What would push it INTO device scope — do not add without advice

- Any dose suggestion, titration hint, or "you should ask your doctor about X".
- Any alert framed clinically ("your symptoms are worsening", "possible
  wearing-off detected") rather than factually ("no dose logged since 08:00").
- Any prediction of future episodes, or a risk score.
- Any automated interpretation of the trend chart into a conclusion.
- Any claim it detects falls, monitors the patient, or can be relied on in an
  emergency.

The **overdue-dose strip is deliberately factual** — it restates the schedule
the user entered and whether a dose was logged. Keep it that way. Rewording it
as clinical advice would change the classification.

## Marketing copy rules (this is where classification is usually lost)

Store listings, screenshots and the website are all "claims". Never write:
detects, diagnoses, monitors, predicts, alerts you to, medical-grade, clinically
validated, reduces falls, improves control.

Safe verbs: records, logs, tracks what you enter, shows, summarises, exports.

## Practical next steps if you distribute in the EU

1. Keep the intended-purpose statement above in the listing and in-app.
2. Get a short written opinion from a regulatory consultant confirming
   non-device status before any paid or clinic-promoted distribution.
3. If a clinician (like the GP who saw it) wants to recommend it to patients,
   that is fine — but do not let promotional material make clinical claims on
   your behalf.
4. Revisit this the moment you add anything interpretive.
